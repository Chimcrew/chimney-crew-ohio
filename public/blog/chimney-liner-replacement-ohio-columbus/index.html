Why smoke backflow happens after a pellet stove install

Pellet stoves are efficient heating appliances, but they rely on correct venting and a properly sized chimney liner to draft combustion gases outside. Smoke backflow occurs when draft is poor or when the existing flue cannot handle the heat or volume the stove produces. Common causes in Columbus homes are:

  • An old clay tile flue that is cracked or partially collapsed, reducing cross section and draft.
  • A lining that is too small for the pellet stove's exhaust, especially if the tenant installed the stove without a professional venting plan.
  • Creosote or ash buildup combined with wet Ohio weather, which limits draft at chimney top.
  • Blockages from bird nests, mortar failure around clay tiles, or a missing chimney cap.

Diagnosing the cause requires a physical inspection and a video scan of the flue to see cracks, offsets, or obstructions. Our services chimney liner repair page explains typical inspection steps and evidence that indicates a full replacement rather than a patch.

Who pays: landlord, tenant, or both

Responsibility depends on three factors: the lease language, whether the tenant installed the stove with permission, and local code or safety violations discovered during inspection.

General rules to apply now:

  • If the tenant installed the pellet stove without written permission and the appliance caused damage to the flue, the tenant may be responsible for repair costs. Keep documentation of the unauthorized installation.
  • If the stove was installed with landlord authorization, or if the flue failed due to age or preexisting defects, responsibility typically falls to you as the landlord. Ohio landlord-tenant law and Columbus housing codes require landlords to maintain safe heating and ventilation systems.
  • If inspection reveals the chimney as a preexisting hazard, you should address it and consider pursuing reimbursement or a deduction from the tenant only after consulting an attorney.

Before committing to payment, document conditions with photos, the video scan, and a written inspection report. What type of liner will solve smoke backflow in Columbus homes

Choices depend on the appliance and chimney condition. For pellet stoves you will most often see these solutions:

  • Insulated stainless steel liner, flexible or rigid, sized for the pellet stove. This is the most durable and code-compliant option for a converted chimney serving a modern appliance.
  • High-temperature stainless liner where the chimney has significant offsets or needs a flexible routing through the flue.
  • Partial relining or spot repair if the problem is limited to a short damaged section and the remainder of the flue is structurally sound.

In Columbus winters you should choose a liner that resists condensation and corrosion. A properly sized stainless steel liner reduces smoke backflow by restoring draft and eliminating gaps where combustion gases can leak into the flue system. How long a chimney liner replacement takes in Columbus

Timeline depends on chimney height, accessibility, and whether masonry repairs are required.

Typical schedules:

  • Simple internal relines with flexible stainless liners: one business day. The crew can remove the old liner, pull the new liner through, set it at the appliance connection, and seal both ends.
  • Relines that require a new roof flashing, a new chimney cap, or a chase cover: one to two business days, because of roof work and weather considerations.
  • Complex jobs requiring dismantling of the crown, rebuilding parts of the flue, or scheduling a mason: two to four days, depending on masonry cure time.

Permit requirements can add time. Columbus sometimes requires permits for significant flue alterations. We handle permit paperwork for landlords to keep the schedule tight. On average, plan for one to two days from start to finish for most Short North rowhouses when no major brickwork is needed.

Can tenants keep using the fireplace or pellet stove until work begins

No. If smoke is backing into the living space, continued use creates immediate health hazards including carbon monoxide exposure and fire risk. You should instruct the tenant to stop using the pellet stove and any fireplace immediately and to ventilate the space by opening windows if safe.

Steps to take right now:

  • Turn off the pellet stove and disconnect power if possible.
  • Close doors to the affected room to limit smoke migration.
  • Arrange an emergency inspection. For landlord responses, call a qualified chimney company such as Chimcrew to perform a safety check and video scan.

You may need to provide temporary heating or an accommodation while repairs proceed, depending on local housing rules and the terms of your lease. Document your actions and any tenant communications.

Cost considerations and how to keep repairs compliant and durable

Costs vary based on liner type, chimney condition, and required masonry work. Ballpark ranges in Columbus for common scenarios are:

  • Flexible stainless liner relining, installed from the roof or basement: typically lower cost and often the most economical, depending on access.
  • Insulated stainless liner installations are higher but provide better condensation resistance and longevity.
  • When crown repair, flashing replacement, and a new cap are required, expect additional material and labor costs.

How to protect your investment:

  • Require code-compliant materials sized for the pellet stove. Improper sizing leads to continued draft problems.
  • Keep records of the inspection, permits, invoices, and photos. These help with insurance claims and protect you in tenant disputes.

Frequently asked questions

If the tenant installed the pellet stove without permission, can the landlord charge them for the liner replacement?

You can potentially charge the tenant if the lease prohibits unapproved appliance installations and the stove directly caused the flue damage. Document the unauthorized installation and the contractor inspection report. Consult a local attorney before making deductions from a security deposit to ensure compliance with Ohio landlord-tenant law.

Will a chimney liner replacement require a building permit in Columbus?

Columbus often requires permits for significant flue modifications, especially when switching liner types or altering roof penetrations. A licensed contractor will know local permit requirements and can handle filings so the work meets code and inspection standards.

How do I know if the chimney needs a full stainless liner instead of a partial repair?

A video inspection will show cracks, collapsed clay tiles, or gaps where combustion gases can escape. If the damage is extensive or the liner cannot be repaired in place, a full stainless steel liner is the safer, longer-lasting solution. The inspection report should explain why a full relining is recommended.
