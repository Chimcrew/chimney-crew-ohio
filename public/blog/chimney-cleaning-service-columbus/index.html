Who pays: tenant responsibility, lease language, and landlord obligations in Columbus

In most cases where a tenant's action causes damage, the tenant is financially responsible. Burning scented wax that produces heavy soot is usually considered tenant-caused damage when it can be shown the tenant used the fireplace or stove in a way that created excessive residues.

What to check now:

  • Review the lease for clauses about fireplace use, prohibitions on certain fuels, and damage or cleaning charges.
  • Check any move-in condition report or inspection photos. Those documents help prove the soot is a new condition caused during the tenancy.
  • Local code and safety: as landlord you are still responsible for ensuring the unit is safe to occupy. That means arranging immediate cleaning or blocking off the appliance if soot or creosote creates a hazard.

Possible outcomes:

  • If the lease specifically forbids burning candles, wax melts, or non-approved fuels, you can charge the tenant for cleaning and any related repairs.
  • If the lease is silent and the damage is ambiguous, you may split the cost or seek recovery through security deposit deductions after following Ohio security deposit law.
  • For repeated or extreme misuse that creates safety risks, you may need to act immediately and then pursue reimbursement.

Document everything: date-stamped photos, a written notice to the tenant about the hazard, and receipts from the cleaning service. Chimcrew can provide a detailed invoice and before-and-after photos from our sweep so you have clear evidence for any deposit deductions or dispute.

Immediate safety steps you should take in Short North and central Columbus apartments

Soot in an appliance flue is not just cosmetic. It can block the draft, cause poor ventilation, and increase carbon monoxide risk.

Do these right away:

  • Do not let the tenant use the fireplace, wood stove, or gas appliance until a professional inspects it.
  • Ventilate the unit, open windows, and run fans to remove lingering smoke odors if the tenant reports respiratory irritation.
  • If you smell strong smoke or suspect a partial blockage, advise the tenant to leave the unit and call the local fire department if there is any active smoke or flame hazard.

What a professional chimney cleaning service will do and why it matters

A professional sweep does more than remove visible soot. The work is a combination of cleaning, inspection, and reporting.

Typical tasks included in a Chimcrew chimney cleaning service:

  • Visual inspection of the firebox, flue, and chimney cap for soot build-up or damage.
  • Mechanical brushing of the flue from the top or the bottom, depending on access, to remove soot and residue.
  • Vacuuming with specialized HEPA-rated equipment to protect the dwelling from dust and fine particulates.
  • Cleaning of the smoke shelf, damper area, and any registers or vents that collected soot.
  • A post-cleaning inspection to check draft and to photograph the cleaned flue.
