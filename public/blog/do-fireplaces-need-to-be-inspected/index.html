Why fireplace inspection matters in Columbus, Ohio

Columbus homes face specific challenges that make regular inspections necessary. Older brick chimneys in Victorian houses around Short North and Victorian Village were built before modern liners became standard. Concrete and mortar expand and contract with Ohio winters, allowing cracks, loose bricks, and water intrusion. You should consider an inspection if your house sits near the river, has mature trees that drop debris into flues, or uses fireplaces heavily during cold snaps.

An inspection identifies hazards such as:

  • creosote buildup that fuels chimney fires
  • cracked flue liners that allow heat and gases to reach combustible materials
  • damaged chimney crowns and caps that let water in and accelerate masonry decay

How often do you need a fireplace inspection

Frequency depends on fuel type, use, and visible problems. Use this practical schedule as a baseline:

  • Wood burning fireplaces: at least once per year, preferably before fall. Heavy users should inspect more often.
  • Gas fireplaces: annually, and after any alteration, migration, or odor of gas. Gas units can develop venting or valve problems that are not obvious.
  • After events: inspect after chimney fires, earthquakes, lightning strikes, or roof work that might have disturbed the chimney.

Inspections are classified by level. A Level 1 inspection is the routine check for appliances and chimneys in normal condition. A Level 2 inspection includes video scanning of the flue and is required for home sales or when changes have occurred. What a fireplace inspection covers

A professional inspection follows a clear checklist, and Chimcrew technicians document findings so you know what needs attention. Typical steps include:

  • Exterior review: condition of chimney crown, cap, mortar joints, and flashing at the roof.
  • Interior review: damper operation, smoke chamber condition, hearth clearances, and visible creosote.
  • Flue evaluation: visual or camera inspection to check liner integrity, obstructions, and creosote classification.
  • Appliance and venting check for gas fireplaces: gas line condition, burners, pilot assembly, and vent terminations.

Inspectors grade creosote and debris, note cracks in the liner or masonry, and recommend repairs such as chimney liner installation, chimney crown replacement, chimney cap replacement, or masonry chimney repair. A documented inspection report helps you prioritize safety work.

Warning signs you need chimney or fireplace repair

Not every problem is obvious. Watch for these signs and call for an inspection if you see them:

  • persistent smoky smell or smoke backdraft into the room
  • visible cracks in the masonry or falling bricks
  • water stains on the ceiling near the chimney or rust on the damper
  • loud noises or popping sounds when the fireplace is in use
  • soot or creosote accumulation that is thick, flaky, or shiny
  • difficulty starting or maintaining gas flame, or the smell of rotten eggs

Repairs might include fireplace repair, chimney crown replacement, or full masonry chimney repair. For wood units we provide targeted services; see our services wood fireplace repair. For gas units with venting or pilot issues, book our services gas fireplace service.
