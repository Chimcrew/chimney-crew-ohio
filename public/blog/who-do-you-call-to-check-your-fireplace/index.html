Winters in central Ohio put chimneys to work from late fall through early spring, and humid summers can hide moisture damage until it becomes a major repair. A certified chimney sweep and inspector will evaluate the entire system, not just the visible firebox. That includes the flue, chimney cap, crown, liner, damper, and any gas components. This article explains who to call, what inspectors look for, and the next steps when problems appear.

Key takeaways

  • Call a certified chimney professional for a fireplace inspection; homeowners should not guess safety issues.
  • Different inspection levels exist: Level 1 is routine, Level 2 is required after changes, Level 3 is invasive.
  • Look for soot buildup, damaged liners, missing chimney caps, and gas appliance problems when deciding to book service.
  • Chimcrew offers free inspection scheduling and covers gas and wood fireplaces throughout Columbus neighborhoods.

Why a professional inspection is the right first call

You may be handy around the house, but chimney and fireplace systems present hidden hazards: creosote buildup, cracked liners, deteriorating mortar, and carbon monoxide risks for gas units. An experienced chimney technician brings training and tools that a homeowner cannot easily match, such as:

  • Video camera flue inspection to see liner condition and obstructions.
  • Combustion and carbon monoxide testing for gas fireplaces.
  • Measurement of creosote levels and structural assessment of the chimney crown and masonry.

Calling a licensed chimney company protects your family and your home value. At minimum, an inspection tells you whether you can continue to use the appliance safely, and if not, what repairs are required.

Which professional do you hire: chimney sweep, HVAC, or mason?

Fireplaces and chimneys overlap trades, so picking the right contractor matters.

  • Chimney sweep and inspection companies specialize in cleaning, inspecting, and repairing flues and liners. They carry the tools needed for safe soot and creosote removal and perform certified inspections.
  • HVAC contractors handle furnace and venting problems, but they are not always equipped for chimney flue work or masonry repairs.
  • Masons are necessary when the chimney structure needs tuckpointing or rebuilding, but they often do not perform flue inspections or gas appliance diagnostics.

For most Columbus homeowners, start with a qualified chimney sweep that offers inspection and repair services. Chimcrew provides chimney cleaning, inspections, and masonry coordination, so you get a single point of responsibility for safety and repairs.

What an inspection covers: Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 explained

Inspectors use three standard levels of chimney inspection. Knowing which applies helps you understand cost and scope.

  • Level 1 inspection: Routine check for a chimney that has not been altered and has had no known problems. The technician examines accessible components, performs a smoke test or simple combustion checks, and may run a camera if needed. Learn more about our level 1 inspection.
  • Level 2 inspection: Required when your system changes or after events that could affect the chimney, such as purchasing or selling a home, or after a chimney fire. This involves a full video scan of the flue to detect hidden damage. See our level 2 inspection.
  • Level 3 inspection: Involves opening portions of the chimney or adjacent construction to inspect concealed areas when a problem is suspected. This is the most invasive and is used only when necessary.

Your inspector will recommend repairs or cleaning based on the findings and will prioritize safety issues like cracked liners or missing chimney caps.

Common problems an inspector will find and how they are fixed

During an inspection, technicians commonly identify these issues and recommend targeted services.

  • Creosote buildup: Requires professional chimney cleaning by a certified sweep to remove deposits that could ignite. This is one of the most frequent reasons for scheduling service.
  • Damaged chimney liner: Cracked or deteriorated liners can allow heat and combustion byproducts into the structure. Solutions include relining with stainless steel or installing a cast-in-place liner.
  • Missing or damaged chimney cap: A cap prevents water, animals, and debris entry. Replacement is a quick, inexpensive repair that prevents larger problems.
  • Deteriorated chimney crown or masonry: Spalling bricks and cracked crowns invite water penetration. Masonry chimney repair and crown replacement restore structural integrity.
  • Gas fireplace issues: For pilot problems, intermittent ignition, or suspected leaks, call a technician who offers both inspection and repair of gas fireplaces. We provide gas fireplace service and testing to ensure safe operation.
  • Wood fireplace repairs: If you have warped grates, cracked fireboxes, or damaged dampers, professional wood fireplace repair ensures safe, efficient burning.

Inspectors prioritize life-safety items, then schedule cosmetic or efficiency improvements. You will receive a clear report and a recommended scope for repair work.

When you must schedule an inspection in Columbus

Book an inspection in these situations to reduce risk and avoid costly repairs later:

  • Before you use the fireplace at the start of the heating season, especially after a year of disuse. Columbus winters and spring thaw cycles can hide damage.
  • After buying or selling a home, a Level 2 inspection is often required by lenders or recommended by home inspectors.
  • After a chimney fire, heavy windstorm, or if you hear cracking or popping from the flue.
  • If you smell strong soot, persistent smoke in the room, or detect higher than normal gas bills for gas fireplaces.
  • If you notice animals, nests, or debris near the chimney top, call for a cap and flue check.

Regular annual inspections for wood-burning fireplaces and periodic checks for gas units keep you compliant with insurance requirements and improve safety.

Common signs that mean you should not use your fireplace until checked

  • Strong, persistent smoke in the room after lighting a fire.
  • Visible cracks in the firebox, flue tile, or liner fragments on the hearth.
  • Strange odors that do not dissipate, especially if you smell rotten eggs or a gas-like scent.
  • Soot accumulating rapidly or deposits that look flaky and brittle.
  • Animals or nests at the chimney top, missing chimney cap, or visible water leaks on the ceiling near the chimney.

If you observe any of these, stop using the fireplace and call a qualified chimney professional immediately.

Frequently asked questions

What is included in a fireplace inspection?

A professional fireplace inspection includes a visual assessment of the firebox, damper, and hearth, a flue examination often with video camera, and checks of the chimney cap, crown, and exterior masonry. For gas fireplaces, technicians test gas connections, pilot assemblies, and combustion products for carbon monoxide. The inspector will report on creosote buildup, liner condition, structural damage, and any immediate safety concerns, then provide a written estimate for cleaning or repairs.

Do fireplaces need to be inspected?

Yes, fireplaces should be inspected. Annual inspections for wood-burning units are standard to identify creosote and liner wear. Gas fireplaces need periodic checks as well to ensure safe combustion and proper venting. In Columbus, seasonal temperature swings and spring moisture can accelerate deterioration, so regular inspections protect your home and meet many insurance requirements.

Who do you call to check your fireplace?

Call a certified chimney sweep and inspection company. They perform cleaning, video flue inspections, and both gas and wood fireplace diagnostics. You can schedule a free inspection with Chimcrew online or by calling (614) 683-5763. We handle chimney sweeping, cap replacement, liner installation, crown repair, and coordinate masonry chimney repair when needed.

How do I tell if my fireplace is safe to use?

Look for warning signs first: heavy smoke in the room, visible cracks in the firebox or flue, brittle or excessive creosote, water stains near the chimney, or any gas odor. Even without these signs, if you have not had a recent inspection, book one. A certified inspector will perform combustion tests, inspect the liner with a camera, and confirm the damper and venting are working properly. If the inspector identifies safety issues, do not use the fireplace until repairs are completed.
